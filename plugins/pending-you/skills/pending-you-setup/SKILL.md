# Set up Pending You

From Pending You’s guide 2.19.0. Read the full guide through Pending You, with `whoami` and `guide` true, so there’s no web fetch to approve (or at https://www.pendingyou.com/skill.md).

The plugin (Claude Cowork and Claude Code) brings the connection to `https://www.pendingyou.com/mcp` and the stub as its skill, so don’t add either yourself. Its setup is a second skill, `pending-you-setup`: your person says “set up Pending You” (in Claude Code, `/pending-you:pending-you-setup` works too), and it does this:

1. Keep one connection. In Claude Code, run `claude mcp list`: a Pending You server whose name doesn’t start with `plugin:` was added by hand, and it overrides the plugin’s. `claude mcp get <name>` shows its scope and the command that removes it. Tell your person to remove it with `claude mcp remove <name> -s <scope>` (usually `claude mcp remove pendingyou -s user`), and to delete a saved `~/.claude/skills/pendingyou` folder, since the plugin carries the skill. Then they restart Claude Code (`/exit`, then `claude --continue`) and say “set up Pending You” again. In Cowork, if Claude’s settings also have a custom Pending You connector, ask them to remove it. Where you can see Pending You’s tools twice (two connections), call `whoami` through each, ask your person to remove the one whose `server` isn’t https://www.pendingyou.com, then to say “set up Pending You” again, and stop.
2. Call `whoami` with your `name`, and check its `server` before anything else: the plugin connects to https://www.pendingyou.com. If `server` is another address, Claude is still connected to another Pending You, and isn’t set up here, whatever `app.setUp` says. Tell your person, with that address: “Claude is still connected to another Pending You, at <server>. Remove that connector in Settings › Connectors. Then connect the plugin’s Pending You connector (Customize › Plugins › Pending You), sign in and click Allow, and say “set up Pending You” again.” In Claude Code, that one is a server added by hand: have them remove it as in step 1. Then stop.
3. `server` is https://www.pendingyou.com and `app.setUp` is true: your app is already set up, maybe on another computer or before a reinstall, so check this computer before you stop. In Cowork, look in your scheduled tasks for one that checks Pending You; in Claude Code, the plugin is all there is, and its tools work. It’s there: introduce yourself in one line, say that when something is theirs to decide you’ll ask here and in Pending You at once, and stop. It’s missing, or your person says they just reinstalled: carry on from step 5.
4. No Pending You tools, or they aren’t signed in: in Cowork, ask your person to open the plugin’s Connectors tab (Customize › Plugins › Pending You), connect Pending You, sign in and click Allow. In Claude Code, ask them to run `/mcp`, choose `plugin:pending-you:pendingyou` and Authenticate. Then they say “set up Pending You” again.
5. Say up front what they’ll approve (see above): in Cowork, “Always allow” for Pending You’s tools, the scheduled task, and “Allow for all scheduled runs” at its first run; in Claude Code, “Yes, and don’t ask again” the first time a Pending You tool asks. Then, in Cowork, create a Cowork scheduled task that checks Pending You every hour, 7 AM–9 PM their time, and ends quietly when nothing is waiting (one already there: keep it rather than adding another), and report `source` “claude” with `hears` “hourly” and its `schedule` (“by-hand” if you couldn’t make one). In Claude Code, report `source` “claude-code”, `name` “Claude Code” and `hears` “while-working”. Report `skillSaved` true: the plugin keeps the skill.
6. Finish as in Report what you set up: `report_setup`, then the test question in `testAreaId`.

## Report what you set up

Right after setup, and whenever how you hear answers changes, call `report_setup` with your `source`, your `name`, `skillVersion` “2.19”, whether the stub is saved for future conversations (`skillSaved`), how you hear answers (`hears`), the check-in you actually created (`schedule`), anything you couldn’t do yourself (`missing`), and, if you can’t open PDFs or text files, `accepts` with what you can (`image`, `pdf`, `text`), so their file picker offers only those. Report only what exists: a stub loaded for this conversation only isn’t saved, and a check-in you only planned isn’t a schedule. `ready` true means you’re configured; if it comes back false, tell them what’s missing in plain words. Where each agent keeps its own check-in (each Grok Bot), report under the name you gave `whoami`: Pending You keeps your check-in under it, and the answer’s `agent.missing` says what of yours is left. Then send them a test question through Pending You (unless your app’s section says your own webhook’s test wake is proof enough), with `areaId` set to the `testAreaId` it returned (Getting started, which tidies itself away once setup is done) and a `session` of just your name, as you gave it to `whoami` (`{ "label": "Wren" }`): no computer name or folder, and never “Setup test”. Right after posting it, tell them in your chat: “Answer it in Pending You, then tell me “done” here.” When they say so, or anything that means they answered, call `get_request` for the test card right away, with your `name`, and pick the answer up with `ack_answer`: don’t leave it for your next check-in, which may be hours away, while their setup page waits on you. If you can keep checking within the same turn while they answer, call `get_request` every `pollAfterSeconds` for up to about 2 minutes, and stop as soon as the answer is there. When their answer arrives, pick it up with `ack_answer`; that makes you verified, and their setup page shows all four steps done: Connected, Checks scheduled, Test delivered, Answer received.
